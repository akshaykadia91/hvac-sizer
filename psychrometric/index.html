1. Getting started

The calculator finds every property of moist air from two known values, runs heating, cooling, humidifying and mixing processes, and draws the result on a psychrometric or Mollier diagram. Your work is saved in this browser automatically.

3. Defining points

A point is fixed by any two different properties: dry bulb, wet bulb, dew point, relative humidity, absolute humidity (W) or enthalpy.

  1. Click New point.
  2. Type a name.
  3. Choose the First property and enter its value.
  4. Choose the Second property and enter its value.
  5. Enter the Airflow (needed for processes).

To edit a point, click its row or dot. If you change a property type, the current value is converted so the point does not move.

4. Using the chart

  • Read any state: hover over the chart. The boxes below show DB, WB, DP, RH, W, h, v and density at the cursor.
  • Move a point: select an input point, then click on the chart. Its two inputs are recalculated. Points made by a process cannot be moved; edit the process.
  • Layers: the chips switch RH, wet bulb, enthalpy (dashed), specific volume and the DB/W grid on or off.
  • Psychrometric or Mollier: Psychrometric puts dry bulb along the bottom (ASHRAE style). Mollier (h-x) puts moisture along the bottom and dry bulb up the side (European style).
  • Marks: a coil with an ADP shows an open circle labelled ADP on the saturation curve. Mixing shows a dashed line between the two streams.

5. Processes

A process takes an entering point, applies a change and creates a new calculated point that updates whenever anything upstream changes.

  1. Set Editing to New process…
  2. Choose Type and Mode.
  3. Choose the Entering point (for mixing, also the With point).
  4. Enter the mode's values and the result name, then click Add process.

Click a process card, or pick it under Editing, to change it. Delete process also removes the point it created.

Heating

Constant moisture. Modes: by heating capacity, or to a leaving dry bulb. From 10 °C / 80 % RH at 1000 CFM, 5 kW gives 18.4 °C and 46.3 % RH; heating to 30 °C needs 11.86 kW.

Cooling coil

Leaving air = BF × entering + (1 − BF) × saturated air at the ADP.

All five modes from one entering point are . Typical bypass factors are 0.05 to 0.20; a deeper coil has a lower BF.

Steam humidification

Steam at 100 °C adds moisture with a small dry-bulb rise. Modes: by steam amount, to leaving RH, to leaving abs. humidity. Use the kg/h result to size the humidifier. See .

Water humidification

Evaporation at nearly constant enthalpy: dry bulb falls, moisture rises. Enter the water temperature. Modes: by saturation efficiency, by water amount, to leaving RH. 85 % efficiency is typical of a good evaporative pad. See .

Mix air

Adiabatic mixing by dry-air mass. Each stream uses its own point's airflow, so change the point airflows to change the ratio. See and .

Choose an Entering and a Leaving point. The panel shows total, sensible and latent load, SHF, moisture removed, ΔT, Δh and dry-air mass flow, using the entering point's airflow. Positive means heat removed. Use it for field checks of installed coils: .

7. Report

  1. Click Report.
  2. Click Copy report (if copying is blocked, the text is selected; press Ctrl+C).
  3. Paste into Word, Excel or an email. Use a fixed-width font such as Consolas so columns line up.

The report lists altitude and pressure, every point, every process with its inputs and results, and the load panel result, in the units currently set. To keep a PDF, paste into Word and save as PDF.

A point with an error has no dot on the chart, and processes using it show an error until it is fixed. Deleting a point that a process uses asks you to click again, then removes the dependent processes too.

9. Formulas and assumptions

ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals equations, worked in SI and converted for display. Saturation pressure by Hyland–Wexler (over ice below 0 °C), range −40 to 90 °C.

p = 101.325 × (1 − 2.25577×10⁻⁵ × Z)^5.2559 kPa, Z in m W = 0.621945 × pw / (p − pw) h = 1.006 t + W (2501 + 1.86 t) kJ/kg dry air v = 287.042 (t + 273.15)(1 + 1.607858 W) / p m³/kg dry air Q total = ṁ (h1 − h2) Q sensible = ṁ (1.006 + 1.86 W2)(t1 − t2) Q latent = Q total − Q sensible ṁ = airflow / v
  • Wet bulb from the ASHRAE psychrometer equation; dew point where saturation pressure equals vapour pressure.
  • Coil: if the ADP is above the entering dew point the coil is dry; if the coil line would enter fog, leaving air is set saturated at the same enthalpy.
  • Steam at 100 °C, 2676 kJ/kg. Spray water enthalpy = 4.186 × water temperature.
  • IP enthalpy on the 0 °F datum (47.8 kJ/kg shows as 28.24 Btu/lb). 1 TR = 3.51685 kW; 1 CFM = 1.699 m³/h; 1 g/kg = 7 gr/lb.
  • Mollier diagram plots t × (1 + 1.849 W) against W.
